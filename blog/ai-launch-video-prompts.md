How to prompt an AI launch video: lessons from 92 real prompts

We read the prompts behind 92 product launch videos made with Claude. The short ones reused a viral prompt. The ones people liked said what to show and how.

Oct 10, 2026

The most-liked launch video in the Prompt Motion gallery, a film for a photo printing app, came from a 912-word prompt. The typical launch prompt in the gallery is 26 words.

Launch and promo videos are the biggest category in the gallery: 94 of 233 entries, 92 of them with the prompt that made them. Here's what those prompts ask for, and what the ones behind the better-received videos do differently.

What a typical AI launch video prompt looks like

More than a quarter of them, 26 of 92, are built on the gallery's most-copied prompt, a request for a 15-second motion design showreel. Some add a product name or URL. Others are the prompt unchanged, so the product must have come from the creator's project or an earlier message. This one made a launch video for Dub:

make a dynamic 15-second motion graphics video that shows what an incredible motion designer you are, like it's your showreel for a résumé. go all out.

It's an easy way to start, and it can work. That run got 394 likes. But the median for the 26 was 8 likes. The prompt asks Claude to show off as a motion designer, so the product is the backdrop, not the subject. Claude has to guess what the product does, which features matter and what the viewer should take away.

What exactly is on screen

The strongest launch prompts name the moments to show. The photo app film asks for 9 to 12 real photos. A prompt for a user-generated ad tool asks for "3 to 5 UI moments to show." The 1,854-word remake prompt asks for "the one-line thing a user types into" the product. Claude builds the video around those, not around a guessed feature list.

Seven of the 92 prompts include an inputs block, a list of things Claude has to ask for before it starts:

Ask me for: the product name and a one-line promise, 3 to 5 UI moments to show, one accent color, 10 to 20 real vertical clips I own, and a royalty-free song with a clear drop (e.g. Mixkit, free for commercial use).

This also makes the prompt reusable. Anyone can run it for their own product without editing it.

What it should look like

Fourteen prompts mention brand colors or the brand itself, and ten include a URL for Claude to study. This one, for an ad in Claude's own visual style, opens with research before any design:

Copy

Make a fast-paced ad for Claude, about 10–15 s (closer to 15). Reference video attached.

1. research:
- Study Claude's earlier brand designs first.
- Find every photo and asset that fits: artistic, editorial, whatever the idea needs.

2. design/direction
- An art reimagine: bold, light mode.
- Clean and perfect: no beige "artsy" look, no eyebrow labels.
- Paint elements on top of the images.
- Above all: fast-paced and very creative.

3. rules
- No slop. Study the reference closely: smoothness, timing, shot durations, fonts, elements.
- Take some inspiration and borrow techniques, but change the design overall, it should look like something you did completely on yourself with your own taste 
- Every morph has to be perfect.

ideas: (since everything moves fast)
- Clean, varied Claude SVG animations
- Images small, bigger, several at once
- Morphing text
- Clean 3D animation
- Zooms

extra notes:
- Feel free to work 10+ hours 
- Give me the final video, ending on the Claude logo.

Show all

How it's timed

Eleven launch prompts mention a beat or a BPM. "Something happens on every beat" gives the edit a structure, and a beat-matched cut is one of the clearest differences between a launch video and a slideshow of features.

What format should a launch video be?

Most of the gallery's launch videos are landscape: 76 of 94, with 12 vertical and 6 square. Yet only 12 of the 92 prompts name a format or a size, so most creators either set it some other way or took what Claude chose. If the video is for a phone-first feed, put "9:16 vertical" and the pixel size in the prompt, so the layout is designed for it from the first frame.

A short launch prompt that does more

You don't need 900 words. Each of these lines replaces a guess:

The product and its promise in one line.

A name and what it does for the user tells Claude more than a URL alone.

Three to five moments to show.

Name real screens or actions, in order.

The look.

Brand colors as hex codes, one font, and light or dark.

The timing.

Length, a tempo, and "something changes on every beat."

What to avoid.

Glows, particle bursts, crossfades and stock-looking icons.

The ending.

The logo, the URL, or one line of copy.
